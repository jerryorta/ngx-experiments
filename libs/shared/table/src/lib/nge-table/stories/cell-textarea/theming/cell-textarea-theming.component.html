NgeTable — Cell Textarea Theming

Every section overrides --nge-table-editor-* on a wrapper class in this story's own SCSS — nothing here reaches into .nge-cell-textarea__*, which would be styling the library rather than theming it. Open a panel in each section: the trigger sits in the table, the panel sits on <body>, and only one mechanism keeps them in step.

1. No overrides — the literal defaults

What a consumer gets having loaded nothing at all. ⚠️ This is the least informative section in the story, and worth saying so: the panel renders correctly here for a reason that has nothing to do with token forwarding — :root defaults reach <body> on their own. A broken forwarding mechanism looks exactly like this section.

2. The accent pair — and the token ARCH-296 had to add

This wrapper sets --nge-table-editor-accent and --nge-table-editor-on-accent. Open a panel: the Apply button is filled with the accent and its label takes the paired foreground.

⚠️ Apply is filled and Cancel is not, deliberately. This editor's whole premise is that the commit is explicit; two identical buttons would put the work of identifying the committing one back on the reader. Filling one is what created the need for an on-accent token at all — every other editor colour sits on a neutral surface.

Try setting only the accent and not its pair, and the label becomes unreadable — which is why the two are bridged together across all ten domain themes rather than one of them.

3. Panel geometry — the other two new tokens

--nge-table-editor-panel-min-width and --nge-table-editor-panel-padding, both widened here. Open a panel and compare it with section 1: it is wider than its cell and roomier inside.

A dropdown is sized to its trigger because its content is short labels. Prose sized to a cell would be unusable, so this panel takes a floor of its own and grows no further — the position strategy has flexible dimensions switched off, so it will not resize while you type.

⚠️ These two are geometry and are deliberately NOT bridged into the domain themes, on the test ARCH-277 set: would a bridge entry teach a contract the table does not honour? A theme says what things look like; how wide a prose editor should be is the consuming column's business.

4. Panel surface separate from field surface

The panel floats above the table while the field sits on it, so --nge-table-editor-panel-surface is a distinct token from --nge-table-editor-surface even when a theme resolves them to the same colour. Here they are pulled apart so the difference is visible.

5. A dark wrapper — the section that actually tests the mechanism

A full dark override on a wrapper class, which is the shape every domain theme takes. Open a panel here. If the table goes dark and the panel stays light, the forwarding is broken — that is the exact failure ARCH-294 shipped and caught, and the reason the token list is now shared and asserted.

⚠️ Check the Unsaved hint and both buttons too, not just the field. A token only one of them reads is the easiest one to leave out of the forwarded list, and it will look perfect in section 1.
